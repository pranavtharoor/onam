# Kerala / Onam / Sadhya reference

Working knowledge for designing and writing accurately. Customs vary by region
(Travancore, Kochi, Malabar), community and family; where this file states a
common practice, treat it as a sensible default, not the only truth. Anything
that will appear as on-screen text or a recognisable depiction should be checked
against a primary source or a Malayali reader. Malayalam strings here are marked
**[verify]** until a native reader has confirmed them in context.

## Onam in time

- Harvest festival of Kerala in **Chingam** (Aug–Sep), the first month of the
  Malayalam calendar, just after the Karkidakam monsoon: the landscape is
  rain-washed, paddy is in, rivers are high, light is clear and golden.
- Ten days from **Atham** to **Thiruvonam** (named for the lunar asterisms).
  **Uthradam**, the day before Thiruvonam, is "first Onam": the shopping rush and
  preparation (*Uthrada paachil*). Thiruvonam is the main day: the Sadhya is lunch.
- Myth: **Mahabali (Maveli)**, a just and generous asura king in whose reign all
  were equal and there was no deceit, was pushed to the netherworld by **Vamana**
  (Vishnu's dwarf avatar) with the third of three promised steps; he was granted
  a yearly visit to his people. Onam is the household making everything ready
  for his return.
- The folk song: *"Maveli naadu vaaneedum kaalam, maanusharellaarum onnupole"* —
  "When Maveli ruled the land, all people were as one." **[verify]**
  മാവേലി നാടു വാണീടും കാലം, മാനുഷരെല്ലാരുമൊന്നുപോലെ.
  The idea of *equality at one table* is the natural emotional core for an
  invitation to a shared meal.

## Pookalam (പൂക്കളം [verify])

- A floral carpet laid on the ground in the front courtyard (*muttam*), made of
  loose petals and flower heads — no coloured powder, no chalk (that is rangoli/kolam).
- Traditionally begun on Atham with a small single ring (often only yellow /
  thumba flowers) and **enlarged each day** with new rings and colours, reaching
  its full size on Thiruvonam. This growth over days is a gift for a scroll story.
- Commonly circular, built in concentric rings with radial segments; modern
  pookalams add intricate geometric and pictorial designs. Petals are placed by
  hand: edges are soft, fills are slightly uneven, colours are flower colours
  (flat, matte, slightly translucent), never neon.
- Clay **Thrikkakara Appan** figures (truncated pyramids, representing Vamana /
  Mahabali) are placed on or beside the pookalam in many homes, especially on Thiruvonam.
- Flowers often named (traditional; many now substituted by market marigold and chrysanthemum):
  - **Thumba** (*Leucas aspera*) — tiny white flowers; the most Onam-specific flower, sung about in Onam songs.
  - **Mukkutti** (*Biophytum sensitivum*) — tiny yellow flowers.
  - **Chethi / Thechi** (*Ixora coccinea*) — red clusters.
  - **Chemparathi** (hibiscus) — red.
  - **Kakkapoovu** (*Utricularia*) — tiny violet-blue flowers of wet paddy fields.
  - **Hanuman kireedam / Krishna kireedam** (*Clerodendrum paniculatum*) — orange-red pagoda flower.
  - **Shankhupushpam** (*Clitoria ternatea*) — deep blue.
  - **Chendumalli** (marigold) — orange/yellow; **Vadamalli** (globe amaranth) — magenta-violet.
  - **Arali** (oleander), **Kolambi** (allamanda, yellow bell), **Aripoo / Konginipoo** (lantana).

## The Sadhya (സദ്യ [verify])

- A vegetarian feast served on a **banana leaf** (*ila*), eaten seated (traditionally
  on a mat in rows), with the right hand. The **leaf's tapering tip points to the
  diner's left**.
- **Geography of the leaf** (common Travancore-style layout; details vary):
  - top-left: salt, banana chips (*upperi*), jaggery-coated chips (*sharkara varatti*), pappadam;
  - left side, upper: pickles — lime (*naranga*), mango (*kadumanga*), ginger-tamarind (*puli inji*);
  - across the top, left→right: *kichadi*, *pachadi* (often sweet pineapple), *thoran*,
    *avial*, *olan*, *kaalan*, *erissery*, *koottu curry*;
  - bottom-left: a small banana (*pazham*);
  - lower centre: **rice** (often Kerala matta, red-flecked), served when the diner sits.
- **Serving sequence** (common): rice with *parippu* (moong dal) and a spoon of
  ghee, with pappadam crushed in → *sambar* → payasam(s) (*ada pradhaman* with
  jaggery and coconut milk; *paal payasam / palada* with milk) → *rasam* →
  *moru* / *pulissery* (buttermilk) to finish.
- Many homes serve 20–30 items; temple feasts (e.g. Aranmula Valla Sadhya) far more.
- Commonly, folding the top half of the leaf **towards yourself** after eating
  signals you were satisfied (conventions differ; don't build the finale on a
  single contested reading without checking with the hosts).
- Cooking vessels: **uruli** (wide, shallow bell-metal/brass pan; also used for
  floating flowers in water), **varpu** (large bell-metal cauldron for payasam),
  **chattukam** (flat spatula), **thavi** (ladle), clay **kalam** and **chatti**.

## Objects

- **Nilavilakku** — tall brass standing lamp with wick(s); lit at dusk and on
  auspicious occasions. Not a clay diya.
- **Kindi** — spouted bell-metal water vessel. **Para** — wooden measure; a
  *nirapara* (para filled with paddy, with a coconut flower spray) marks auspicious occasions.
- **Onakkodi** — new clothes for Onam. **Kasavu** — off-white handloom cotton
  with a gold zari border (*kasavu mundu*, *set-mundu*, *set-saree*). The gold is a
  narrow band, sometimes with a thin coloured stripe.
- **Oonjal** — rope swing hung from a tree branch for Onam.

## Architecture

- **Nalukettu** — traditional house of four wings around an open central
  courtyard (**nadumuttam**) that lets rain and sky into the house; larger
  forms: *ettukettu*. **Padippura** — the roofed gatehouse at the compound entrance.
  **Poomukham** — front verandah; **charupadi** — wooden bench-railing along it.
- Steep sloping roofs of clay tile (or thatch, historically), deep eaves, carved
  wooden pillars and brackets, laterite walls, red-oxide floors that are cool
  and slightly glossy.
- The sequence *gatehouse → front yard (muttam) with pookalam → verandah →
  courtyard open to the sky → dining hall* is a ready-made camera path.

## Landscape

Coconut palms (*thengu*) with arching fronds; paddy fields (*vayal*, *padam*)
in bright wet green; backwaters (*kayal*) with still reflective water, Chinese
fishing nets in Kochi; laterite red earth; monsoon-heavy clouds clearing to Chingam sun.

## Celebrations (use sparingly; each is its own world)

Vallam kali (snake-boat races, *chundan vallam*), Pulikali (painted tiger dancers,
Thrissur), Thiruvathira kali (women's circle dance around a lamp), Kaikottikali,
Kummattikali (masked dancers), Onathallu, Uriyadi. One, chosen for the story,
beats a montage of all.
